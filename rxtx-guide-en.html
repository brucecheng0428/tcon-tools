1About this tool

A panel timing spec lands on your desk and you have to answer straight away: can the interface carry this resolution and refresh rate? This tool puts the frequency math for four interfaces on one page. The four cards share one set of Frame parameters, so changing a single number updates all four at once.

What it calculates

  • TCON Rx (input side) - the clock frequency at each LVDS Rx stage, and the Bit Rate per Lane for P2P (eDP)
  • TCON Tx (output side) - the mLVDS Tx frequency, and the Tx Rate for P2P (iSP / PHI)
  • It also reports 1 UI (the duration of a single bit) and 1/16 UI, the numbers you need most often when sizing a skew budget or a measurement resolution
  • For eDP it also tells you which Link Rate tier you need at minimum (RBR / HBR / HBR2 / HBR3)

Three things to know first

  • There is no "Calculate" button - every field computes live, and all four cards recalculate as you type.
  • All four cards share the Frame parameters - Hactive / Hblank / Vactive / Vblank on the top card are the common basis of every formula.
  • Frame Rate is linked in both directions - the Frame Rate on LVDS Rx and P2P Rx stay in sync with each other, and the two Tx cards reuse the same value. See section 9.
The results are estimates What you get here are theoretical frequencies. They exclude the real PHY margin, board losses and the IC spec ceiling. To decide whether a given IC can carry the load, follow the official datasheet. This tool is for comparing configurations quickly and catching order-of-magnitude mistakes.

2Quick start

Walk through "1920×1080 @144Hz, 4 Port LVDS in, 4 Port mLVDS out" in 30 seconds.

Pick a resolution

In the Res. dropdown at the top right of the Basic Parameters card, pick 1920x1080. Hactive / Hblank / Vactive / Vblank are filled in at once, and Htotal and Vtotal appear immediately.

Pick a calculation mode on the LVDS Rx card

Four modes, one choice: pick whichever number you already have. Here choose Given Frame Rate, enter 144, and set Port to 4.

Read the Rx results

Pixel Rate, FPGA UI DCLK, TCON UI DCLK, LVDS per Port and 1 UI all appear at once. On an eDP interface, read "Rx Bit Rate / Lane" on the P2P Rx card and the eDP spec suggestion below it instead.

No need to enter Frame Rate again on the Tx side

The two TCON Tx cards below reuse the same Frame Rate automatically. Just choose Driver bits / Port / pairs and the Tx frequency appears.

Add the boost when you want the worst case

In "DCLK Frequency Boost" at the bottom of the LVDS Rx card, tick SSC spread and OSC deviation. Each result then shows a maximum value in parentheses beside it.

Why you cannot guess at Blank

Every frequency is built on Htotal × Vtotal × Frame Rate, and Htotal / Vtotal include blank. Fill in active but not blank and the calculated frequency comes out about 8–15% too low - that is the classic way to believe you have margin when the link will not actually run. Copy the blank values from the panel datasheet; do not work from memory.

A bigger Blank is not safer either Enlarging Blank pushes up Pixel Rate and every downstream frequency with it. When you trade specs off, every extra 100 pixels here raises the results on all four cards.

Results and formulas

ResultFormulaWhat it means
Frame RatePixel Rate × 10⁶ ÷ (Htotal × Vtotal)The actual refresh rate. It is a computed result only in the reverse-solve modes.
Pixel RateHtotal × Vtotal × Frame Rate ÷ 10⁶, or FPGA DCLK × PortThe number of pixels sent per second (MHz), the central intermediate value of the whole page.
FPGA UI DCLKPixel Rate ÷ PortThe pixel clock of each Port on the signal source side.
TCON UI DCLKFPGA UI DCLK × 2The internal TCON pixel clock, always twice the value above.
LVDS per Port= FPGA UI DCLKThe clock frequency of each LVDS Port. This is the one your measurement should match.
1 UI [ps]10⁶ ÷ (7 × LVDS per Port)The duration of a single bit. The factor 7 comes from the 7:1 serialization of LVDS: seven bits go out in one clock period.
What 1 UI is for It is the denominator of the skew / jitter budget. At 1 UI = 680 ps, for example, a 100 ps trace mismatch on the board already eats about 15% of the eye width. 1 UI shrinks fast as resolution goes up, which is why the same board only starts to fail at high refresh rates.

5DCLK Frequency Boost (Max Estimate)

It sits at the bottom of the LVDS Rx card. It answers one question: with every factor that raises the frequency taken into account, how bad is the worst case? Design margin is judged on this number, not on the nominal value.

The three checkboxes

ItemTypePercentage options / defaultWhen to tick it
RX SSC SpreadCheckbox + dropdown±0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0%, default ±3.0%The signal source runs spread spectrum (SSC). Spreading swings the instantaneous frequency above and below the nominal value, and the worst case takes the upper edge.
OSC Process DeviationCheckbox + dropdown±0.5 – 3.0%, default ±2.0%The internal oscillator drifts with process and temperature. Include it when you assess the internal clock margin of the IC.
TX SSC SpreadCheckbox + dropdown±0.5 – 3.0%, default ±3.0%Tick this when the output side runs spread spectrum too.

How the boost is computed

The ticked items are multiplied together, not added:

Max = nominal × (1+a%) × (1+b%) × (1+c%)

With all three ticked at 3% / 2% / 3%, the factor is about 1.0815, that is 8.15% above nominal. With none ticked, every hint in parentheses disappears.

Which results get the boost

Result fieldWhich boosts apply
TCON UI DCLKAll three (whichever are ticked)
LVDS per PortRX SSC Spread only
LVDS 1 UIRX SSC Spread only
mLVDS Tx Freq / 1 UI / 1/16 UIAll three
P2P Tx Rate / BK Freq / 1 UI / 1/16 UIAll three

The boosted value is shown in parentheses next to the original number, and "Boosted Max DCLK" is listed on its own below the boost block.

Why LVDS per Port ignores OSC and TX SSC This is the real on-wire frequency on the input side, so only the spread of the signal source affects it. OSC deviation happens inside the IC and TX SSC happens on the output side; neither changes the LVDS frequency coming in. The distinction is deliberate, not an omission.

TCON Rx LaneRadio buttons1 / 2 (default) / 4The number of Lanes. Double the Lanes and the Bit Rate per Lane halves - when the result exceeds the top tier, this is the first thing to change.

Formula

Rx Bit Rate / Lane [Gbps] = Htotal × Vtotal × Frame Rate × Color Depth × 3 × (Enc Out ÷ Enc In) ÷ (Lane × 10⁹)

  • × 3: the three RGB channels
  • Enc Out ÷ Enc In: the encoding overhead, 10/8 = 1.25 for eDP
  • ÷ Lane: the data is split evenly across the Lanes

How the two Tx cards differ On the mLVDS card, bits are divided by 2 (DDR) and pairs by 3. On the P2P card, bits multiply directly and pairs divide directly, plus there is the Encoding overhead. What you changedWhat the tool does
Change the Frame Rate on P2P RxIt switches the LVDS Rx mode to "Given Frame Rate" automatically, fills in the same value, then recalculates every LVDS result. This is deliberate: the two sides cannot have different refresh rates.

So "I changed the eDP FPS and the LVDS mode jumped on its own" is normal It is not a mis-click. If you want to keep FPGA DCLK as the input, leave the Frame Rate field on P2P Rx alone - touching it switches the mode away.

10FAQ

Click a question to open the answer.

Both Tx cards show — and nothing gets calculated?▼

The Tx cards have no Frame Rate of their own, so the shared value has to be valid first. Work out a Frame Rate on either the LVDS Rx or the P2P Rx card (or just type one into P2P Rx) and the Tx numbers appear at once. Also, nothing on the page is calculated while Htotal or Vtotal is 0.

The Encoding fields are greyed out and will not change?▼

They are locked on purpose: when I/F is eDP (8/10), iSP (8/9) or PHI (10/12), Encoding is fixed by the spec and should not be changed casually. To set your own values, switch I/F to Other and both fields unlock.

The calculated frequency is well below what I measured?▼

Work through these in order of likelihood:

  1. Blank is missing or too small - the most common cause; see section 3.
  2. The SSC / OSC boost was left out - a measurement captures the instantaneous maximum, so of course the nominal value is lower. Tick the boosts from section 5 and compare again.
  3. The Encoding overhead was ignored - 8b/10b on eDP is exactly 25%.
  4. You measured the frequency at a different node (taking TCON DCLK for LVDS per Port, for example, which differ by a factor of two).
Why does 1 UI use ×7 for LVDS but ×2 for mLVDS?▼

The two serialize differently. LVDS uses 7:1 serialization, seven bits per clock period. mLVDS uses DDR, one bit on the rising edge and one on the falling edge, so twice the rate. The factor reflects the real bit density, not a unit conversion.

Minimum eDP Spec shows the out-of-spec warning in red?▼

It means the calculated Bit Rate per Lane already exceeds the top tier in the table (10.0 Gbps). Your options: add Lanes (4 Lanes halves the rate per Lane), lower the Color Depth, shrink blank, or lower the refresh rate. All four are spec trade-offs; change one and come back to the numbers on this card.

Can I put these numbers straight into a datasheet?▼

Not recommended. What you get here are theoretical frequencies, without the real IC spec ceiling, the PHY margin or board losses. They are good for comparing configurations quickly and checking orders of magnitude; for official numbers, follow the datasheet and your own measurements.
